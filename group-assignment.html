Present & Past Simple

  1. JUAN FRANCISCO GARCIA VALERIO
  2. ASHLEY GUZMAN CASTILLO
  3. IVANIA GUADALUPE GUZMAN SORIANO

Guiding questions

  • When do we use the present simple for habits and facts, and the past simple for finished actions?
  • How do we form affirmative sentences, negatives and questions in each tense?
  • What changes with he / she / it? Why do we use the base verb after does, doesn’t, did and didn’t?
  • How do regular and irregular verbs change in the past? Give one example of each.

Model examples: “She studies every day.” / “She studied yesterday.” “She doesn’t study on Sundays.” / “She didn’t study yesterday.” “Does she study?” / “Did she study?”

Simple visual: Create a two-column chart: Every day / Yesterday. Highlight the verbs and time expressions. Include one habit, one finished action and one irregular verb pair such as go → went.

Share the speaking time: Member 1 explains present simple; Member 2 explains past simple; Member 3 compares the forms and leads the class question. Each member gives at least one example.

Ask the class: “Every day, she ___ English. Yesterday, she ___ English.” Use study and explain both answers.

GROUP 3 · GRAMMAR STUDIO

Future Actions: Will & Going To

  1. MARFEL SOFIA MARTINEZ LOPEZ
  2. ANDRY MARIA MATEO AMADOR
  3. FRANCHESCA MORENO VICTORIANO

Guiding questions

  • How do we form affirmative sentences, negatives and questions with will and be going to?
  • When can we use will for a decision made now, an offer or a promise?
  • When can we use going to for an earlier plan or a prediction based on present evidence?
  • Which future time expressions can we use, and why does going to need am, is or are?

Model examples: “I’ll help you.” “I won’t be late.” “Will you call tomorrow?” “We are going to study tonight.” “She isn’t going to travel.” “Are you going to practice?”

Simple visual: Create a Will / Going to comparison chart and a short dialogue. Show a decision made now, a plan made earlier and a prediction with visible evidence.

Share the speaking time: Member 1 explains will; Member 2 explains be going to; Member 3 compares the situations and leads the class question. Each member gives at least one example.

Ask the class: “We booked our tickets yesterday. We ___ travel next week.” Complete the sentence using be going to and explain your choice.

Remember: Will and going to can sometimes overlap. Explain the context instead of treating the difference as an absolute rule.
