02 / SEE THE DIFFERENCE

Same person. Different time.

The verb form changes with the meaning. Time expressions help, but always read the whole message.

PAST SIMPLE

Yesterday

Maya walked to class.

A finished action before now.
PRESENT SIMPLE

Every morning

Maya walks to class.

A regular habit, not necessarily happening now.
FUTURE · GOING TO

Tomorrow

Maya is going to walk to class.

A plan she has already made.
What about right now?

For an action happening at this moment, use the present continuous: “Maya is walking to class now.” This lesson focuses on present simple habits and facts.

SENTENCE REPAIR

Find the mistake. Make it right.

Say the corrected sentence before revealing the answer.

She don’t study on Sundays.

She doesn’t study on Sundays.
Use doesn’t with he, she and it. Keep study in its base form.

Did you went to class?

Did you go to class?
After did, use the base verb: go.

I will to call you tomorrow.

I will call you tomorrow.
Use will + base verb, without to.

04 / MAKE IT YOURS

Your life, in three moments.

Work with a partner. Prepare three true sentences, then ask follow-up questions.

A HABIT

Every week, I…

“Every week, I study English.”

Ask: What do you do after class?
A MEMORY

Last weekend, I…

“Last weekend, I visited a friend.”

Ask: Where did you go?
A PLAN

Next weekend, I’m going to…

“I’m going to practice with a partner.”

Ask: What are you going to do?
YOUR EXIT TICKET

Write one habit, one finished action and one future plan. Underline each verb and circle the time expression.

Teacher’s quick guide

Suggested sequence: warm-up (5 min), grammar exploration (15 min), comparison (5 min), practice and sentence repair (15 min), partner speaking (10 min). Model each form before asking learners to produce it.

Check for: meaning, subject–verb agreement, base verbs after auxiliaries and a clear time reference. Accept alternative correct sentences during speaking. The practice score records only first attempts and resets when the page reloads.
